The blue sky during the day is caused by a phenomenon known as **Rayleigh scattering**. This process occurs because sunlight, which contains various wavelengths of light, interacts with the molecules and small particles in the Earth's atmosphere. When sunlight passes through the atmosphere, shorter wavelengths (like blue and violet) are scattered in all directions more efficiently than longer wavelengths (like red and yellow). In clear air, this scattered light scatters equally, giving the sky its blue appearance. However, as the light travels farther into space, the shorter wavelengths get absorbed or scattered more strongly by molecules and dust particles. At sunrise and sunset, the Sun is much lower in the sky, so the sunlight must travel through a much larger volume of the atmosphere. This means more sunlight is scattered in all directions simultaneously, including back toward the observer. The longer wavelengths dominate, so the sky appears brighter blue and less saturated with colors. Solar pollution also plays a role. Pollutants and aerosols such as dust, soot, or sulfate particles scatter and absorb sunlight, especially in the lower atmosphere where they can block the Sun’s rays from reaching the horizon. These particles often have shorter wavelengths, making the sun appear warmer and more golden, while underlying dust scatters more blue light, shifting the sky’s color toward orange or reddish tones as well. In summary: - Blue sky → Rayleigh scattering → scattered short wavelengths dominate. - Sunset → Sun is lower in the sky → longer wavelengths dominate → sky appears orange or red. - Dust/pollution → scattered/absorbed longer wavelengths → sky becomes more muted and reddish.
