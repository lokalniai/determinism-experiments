reference: A100, E1 greedy, r0 · 226 tokens · no run identical
The sky appears blue during the day due to a phenomenon called Rayleigh scattering. This occurs when sunlight interacts with the Earth's atmosphere, and shorter wavelengths of light (like blue and violet) are scattered more efficiently than longer wavelengths (like red and orange). The scattering is much stronger for blue light because it has a shorter wavelength, which is more easily scattered by atmospheric particles. Sunset times are often red or orange because the sun is lower in the sky, and the scattering of sunlight by the atmosphere is more pronounced during this time. As the sun sets, the sunlight travels through more of the atmosphere, scattering the shorter blue and violet light more than the longer wavelengths. This leaves the longer wavelengths, such as red and orange, to dominate the sky's colors. Atmospheric conditions like dust or pollution can also affect the appearance of the sky. Dust particles can scatter sunlight in a way that makes the sky appear darker or more muted, while pollution can introduce additional colored particles that alter the sky's appearance. However, these effects are generally less noticeable than the impact of Rayleigh scattering on the blue light.
