The Physics of Rayleigh Scattering #### The Nature of Sunlight Sunlight appears white, but it is actually a mixture of all colors in the visible spectrum. These molecules are much smaller than the wavelengths of visible light. When sunlight enters the atmosphere, it collides with these gas molecules. According to **Rayleigh's Law**, the intensity of scattered light ($I$) is inversely proportional to the fourth power of the wavelength ($\lambda$): $$ I \propto \frac{1}{\lambda^4} $$ This mathematical relationship means that shorter wavelengths (blue and violet) are scattered much more strongly than longer wavelengths (red and orange). Specifically, blue light is scattered roughly 10 times more effectively than red light. ### 2. Why the Sky is Blue (Daytime) During the middle of the day, the sun is high in the sky, and sunlight travels through a relatively thin layer of the atmosphere to reach your eyes. 1. **Scattering:** As sunlight hits the nitrogen and oxygen molecules, the short-wavelength blue light is scattered in all directions (up, down, and sideways). 2. **Perception:** When you look up at any part of the sky (away from the sun), you are seeing this scattered blue light entering your eyes. 3. **Why not Violet?** Although violet light has an even shorter wavelength than blue and is scattered even more, the sky does not appear violet for two reasons: * **Solar Spectrum:** The sun emits significantly more blue light than violet light. * **Human Vision:** The human eye is much more sensitive to blue light than violet light. **Conclusion:** The combination of strong scattering of short wavelengths and human visual sensitivity results in the sky appearing blue. ### 3. Why Sunsets are Red or Orange Sunsets occur when the sun is near the horizon. In this position, sunlight must travel through a much thicker layer of the atmosphere to reach the observer compared to when the sun is overhead. 1. **Increased Path Length:** The path length through the atmosphere can be up to 30–40 times longer at sunset than at noon. 2. **Cumulative Scattering:** Because the path is so long, almost all the short-wavelength blue and green light is scattered out of the direct beam of sunlight before it reaches your eyes. 3. **Transmission of Long Wavelengths:** The longer wavelengths (red, orange, and yellow) are scattered much less. Therefore, they pass through the thick atmosphere relatively unimpeded. 4. **Result:** The light that finally reaches your eyes directly from the sun is depleted of blue/green and dominated by red/orange hues. ### 4. It scatters all colors of light relatively equally. * **Effect on Color:** * **Haze/Clouds:** If the atmosphere is full of large water droplets or dust, Mie scattering dominates. This scatters all colors together, often washing out the deep blue of the sky and making it appear white or pale gray. * **Enhanced Sunsets:** Paradoxically, certain pollutants can make sunsets more vibrant.
