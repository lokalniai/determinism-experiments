The sky appears blue during the day due to the scattering of sunlight by the Earth's atmosphere. This phenomenon occurs because the atmosphere contains tiny water droplets and dust particles. These particles act as tiny reflectors for sunlight, bouncing it forward and sideways in a process called Rayleigh scattering. Blue light, with its shorter wavelength, scatters more efficiently through the air than other colors like red or orange. This makes the sky appear blue. Atmospheric conditions play a significant role in sunset colors. For sunset hues to occur, the sun must be low in the sky, usually at or below the horizon. In this condition, the sunlight must pass through more of the atmosphere and undergo multiple scattering, resulting in a much warmer, reddish-orange color. This extended atmospheric path length allows more blue light to be scattered back toward Earth, enhancing the overall color. Conversely, if the sun is high or directly overhead, it appears much bluer due to reduced scattering, often remaining clear or slightly pinkish. Other atmospheric factors, such as pollution, dust, or particulates, can also affect a sunset's color. Pollution can contain absorbing particles that scatter light differently than scattering it. Dust, for example, can be particularly effective at scattering short wavelengths like blue light. Therefore, the presence or absence of these particles can make a sunset either redder or more orange.
