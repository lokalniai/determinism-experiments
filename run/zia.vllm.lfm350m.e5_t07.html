The sky appears blue during the day due to a process called Rayleigh scattering. This phenomenon occurs when sunlight interacts with the Earth's atmosphere. When sunlight enters the atmosphere, it encounters tiny molecules of gases like nitrogen and oxygen. These molecules are much smaller than the light waves that reach us, so they scatter them in all directions, including from the blue wavelengths. The scattering is more intense when the light is shorter in wavelength, meaning blue light is scattered more effectively than other colors like red or orange. Sunsets are often red or orange because of the same atmospheric process, but at a different wavelength. As the sun dips below the horizon, the sunlight that has already scattered blue light has been redirected into the atmosphere, where it is scattered by larger particles and molecules in the air. This scattering of red light is what makes sunsets appear red or orange. The red light has longer wavelengths and is less scattered, so it dominates the sky's colors as the sun moves lower in the sky. Atmospheric conditions such as dust, pollution, and water vapor can also affect the appearance of the sky and sunsets. Dust particles can scatter light in a way that makes the sky appear darker and more red, while pollution can introduce haze that scatters light in all directions, making the sky appear hazy or brownish. Water vapor can also scatter light, though to a lesser extent than dust or pollution, contributing to a more uniform but still red or orange appearance. These factors combine to create the dynamic and ever-changing colors of the sky during sunsets.
