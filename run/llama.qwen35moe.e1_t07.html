Rayleigh Scattering (Why the Sky is Blue) The Earth’s atmosphere is composed mainly of nitrogen ($N_2$) and oxygen ($O_2$) molecules. These molecules are much smaller than the wavelengths of visible light. When sunlight enters the atmosphere, it collides with these gas molecules. According to **Rayleigh’s Law**, the intensity of scattered light ($I$) is inversely proportional to the fourth power of its wavelength ($\lambda$): $$ I \propto \frac{1}{\lambda^4} $$ This equation has two critical implications: 1. **Shorter wavelengths scatter much more strongly.** Blue light (~450 nm) has a shorter wavelength than red light (~650 nm). Thus, when you look up at the sky (away from the sun), you see this scattered blue light. The sky appears blue rather than violet (which has an even shorter wavelength) because: - The sun emits less violet light than blue light. - The human eye is more sensitive to blue light than violet light. ### 3. Sunlight travels through a relatively thin layer of atmosphere to reach your eyes. While some blue light is scattered away, enough of it still reaches your eyes directly, and the scattered blue light dominates the view of the sky. - **At Sunset/Sunrise**: The sun is near the horizon.
