The sky appears blue during the day due to a phenomenon called Rayleigh scattering. This occurs when sunlight interacts with the Earth's atmosphere, and shorter wavelengths (like blue and violet) are scattered more efficiently than longer wavelengths (like red and orange). The scattering is much stronger for blue light because it has a shorter wavelength, which is more easily scattered by air molecules and aerosols. Sunset times are often red or orange because the Sun is lower in the sky, and the scattering of sunlight by the atmosphere is most pronounced during this time. As the Sun sets, the sunlight that reaches the Earth's surface is scattered in all directions, including back toward the ground. The longer wavelengths of light, such as red and orange, are scattered less and thus appear more vivid. This is why sunset colors are often described as warm and vibrant. Atmospheric conditions such as dust, pollution, or water vapor can significantly alter the colors of the sky. Dust particles can scatter light in all directions, potentially making the sky appear hazy or gray. Pollution can also affect the quality of the light reaching the Earth's surface, often leading to a more muted or reddish appearance. Water vapor, on the other hand, can absorb certain wavelengths of light, particularly red and orange, making the sky appear more muted or dark. These changes in atmospheric composition can make sunset colors appear more intense and vivid.
